Mars Polar Ice Caps
The polar regions of Mars are covered by permanent ice caps composed of both water ice and frozen carbon dioxide. The northern cap is mostly water ice, while a thinner seasonal layer of carbon dioxide frost forms and sublimates each Martian year. The southern cap sits atop a thicker permanent layer and is colder, allowing carbon dioxide ice to persist. Measurements from orbit show that the combined polar deposits contain enough water ice to cover the planet in a global ocean tens of meters deep if melted. The layered structure of the caps records climate cycles, much like ice sheets on Earth, and scientists study these layers to understand how the Martian climate has changed over millions of years. Understanding the polar caps matters for future exploration because they represent an accessible source of water for human missions.
