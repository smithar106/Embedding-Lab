Ancient Water on Mars
For decades scientists have searched for signs that liquid water once flowed across the surface of Mars. Orbital observations from spacecraft have identified minerals that typically form only in the presence of liquid water, including clays and hydrated sulfates. These minerals are found in ancient terrains, suggesting that Mars was once far wetter than it is today. River-like channels and deltas, visible from orbit, appear to have been carved by flowing water billions of years ago. In addition, rovers on the ground have examined sedimentary rocks that show evidence of having been laid down by ancient streams and lakes. The Curiosity rover, exploring Gale Crater, found rounded pebbles and layered mudstones consistent with a long-lived lake environment. More recently, the Perseverance rover has been collecting samples from an ancient river delta in Jezero Crater, a site chosen precisely because it preserves a record of past water. Radar soundings have also detected what may be a large reservoir of liquid water beneath the southern polar ice cap. Taken together, the mineral, morphological, and rover evidence makes a strong case that Mars hosted liquid water in its distant past, even though its surface today is cold and dry.
