James Webb Space Telescope
The James Webb Space Telescope is a large infrared observatory launched to observe the most distant galaxies and study the early universe. Its segmented mirror and sunshield allow it to detect faint infrared light that is invisible to telescopes operating in visible wavelengths. Webb observes stellar nurseries, exoplanet atmospheres, and the formation of the first galaxies after the Big Bang. Because infrared light penetrates cosmic dust, Webb can see regions that are opaque to other instruments, providing a new view of star and planet formation across the universe. Its scientific goals are focused on cosmology and astrophysics rather than the geology of nearby planets.
